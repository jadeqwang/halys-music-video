# HALYS: on-screen fact check (v1, 3 Oct 2026)

Scope: every on-screen claim in `SHOTLIST.md` ("On-screen facts to verify") and the terminal in `ROOM.md`. The standard: it has to survive someone pausing the frame and replying with a source.

**How it was checked.** The session's egress proxy blocked direct page fetches from almost every host (NASA, Wikipedia, publishers). Facts were confirmed in three ways: (1) search-engine extracts of the pages linked below; (2) primary texts from the Perseus TEI on GitHub (Godley's Herodotus and the Greek); (3) our own ephemeris runs. For those runs, 2026–27 used Skyfield 1.55 with DE421. For 585 BC we used the DE422 package from RESEARCH, with contacts found by root-finding rather than the 1-s sampling RESEARCH used. Before render, a human should click through the four links that carry changed numbers (marked ★).

## Other claims in SHOTLIST.md and ROOM.md that looked shaky

1. **S70 and P37, Karnak "at dusk".** At Luxor, totality is around midday (10:02–10:08 UT) with the Sun **82° up**, almost overhead. A low black Sun behind the columns is wrong. Frame it looking straight up between the column capitals of the Hypostyle Hall, and drop "at dusk" from P37.
2. **ROOM beat 5, "the band of totality sits squarely over the Halys".** A shift of +300 s only clips the south bend, near the band's northern edge (1 m 19 s). Centring the band needs about +700 to +1,000 s, roughly 1.5–2σ: the bend then gets 3 m 08 s to 3 m 30 s, and Kırıkkale only goes total from about +800 s. Draw the band to match the number shown, or change the number.
3. **S37 HUD `C2 · TOTALITY · 00:00:07` counting up.** It must never pass the totality length you claim: 2:16 at the representative site, or 1:19 at the river with +300 s.
4. **S03 `AND BOTH ARMIES WENT HOME.`** Herodotus says they *stopped fighting* and grew eager for peace (1.74.3). "Went home" is inference. It is low risk, but `AND BOTH ARMIES STOPPED FIGHTING.` is word-for-word defensible.
5. *(v2: removed; see "Thales" below.)* **S53 `SAROS · 18 Y 11 D 8 H` floating around Thales.** It reads as "Thales used the saros", which RESEARCH §7 lists under "do not say": it is unproven, and Halley gave the cycle its name in 1691. The figure itself is right (6,585.32 d; 10 or 11 days depending on leap years). Keep it as decoration only, never as a caption.
6. *(v2: removed; see "Thales" below.)* **S53 forecast card, "over the Halys".** Under standard ΔT the Halys reached only 96–99 % (RESEARCH §1.5), so the card is only "true" in the world after her ΔT drag. That is fine as a joke, but nothing else on screen should assert totality at the Halys as fact.
7. **ROOM sticker `1420.405 MHz`.** The hydrogen line is 1420.405752 MHz, which rounds to 1420.406. Either `1420.4058 MHz` or `1420 MHz` avoids the truncation reply.
8. **ROOM `treaty: border=halys`.** Herodotus says the Halys was the frontier (1.72). He does not say the treaty set it, so this is the standard modern inference. Fine in a joke.
9. **S01 `28 MAY 585 BC`** is Julian, the historians' convention; it is 22 May proleptic Gregorian. Fine, as long as no Gregorian or ISO date appears beside it (see the terminal fix).
10. **Same error elsewhere in the docs:** ZEITGEIST §2.3 ("earliest historical event whose exact date is known") repeats the error fixed in #2. RESEARCH §1.5's threshold ("the southern bend enters totality from ΔT ≈ 18,700 s") is slightly conservative: our re-run finds about 18,610 s (+230 s). Its 18,800 s row checks out (2 m 08.7 s at Avanos).

## The oath (S79, v2 decision 7)

On screen in S79 (270.04–273.40): Alyattes and Cyaxares clasp right hands on the final chord. Each bare forearm has one thin fresh cut, and the two mediators stand behind. One caption: PLAQUE `THE OATH · HERODOTUS 1.74` (drawn by `video/src/scenes/treaty.js`).

**Primary text, re-checked 3 Oct 2026** against Godley 1920 (the Perseus-modernised TEI) and the Perseus Greek TEI, both on GitHub:

> **1.74.3** "…Those who reconciled them were Syennesis the Cilician and Labynetus the Babylonian;"
> **1.74.4** "they brought it about that there should be a sworn agreement and a compact of marriage between them: they judged that Alyattes should give his daughter Aryenis to Astyages, son of Cyaxares; for without strong constraint agreements will not keep their force."
> **1.74.5** "These nations make sworn compacts as do the Greeks; and besides, when they cut the skin of their arms, they lick each other's blood."

The Greek of 1.74.5 is ὅρκια δὲ ποιέεται ταῦτα τὰ ἔθνεα τά πέρ τε Ἕλληνες, καὶ πρὸς τούτοισι, ἐπεὰν τοὺς βραχίονας ἐπιτάμωνται ἐς τὴν ὁμοχροίην, τὸ αἷμα ἀναλείχουσι ἀλλήλων. Literally: "…and in addition, when they cut their arms into the surface skin, they lick up one another's blood." ἐς τὴν ὁμοχροίην means only into the skin, so a thin, shallow cut is the faithful picture.

| 5 | The right-hand clasp | **Our staging, defensible** | Herodotus describes no gesture beyond "as do the Greeks" plus the blood. The clasped right hand was the standard pledge in the Greek world (δεξιά, the *dexiosis* of Greek reliefs). It is also attested in the Near East: the throne dais of Shalmaneser III from Fort Shalmaneser, Nimrud (c. 846 BC, Iraq Museum), shows the Assyrian king clasping hands with Marduk-zakir-shumi I of Babylon. Diodorus 16.43.4 calls the right hand "the surest pledge amongst the Persians", a later and Persian-era source (seen via a search extract only). A forearm grip has no ancient attestation, so the shot uses a hand clasp. |

He does not narrate the ceremony or say who swore. Kings as the oath-takers is the natural reading, not a reported scene. |
