# HALYS — treatment (v0, director's draft)

> **28 May 585 BC.** Two armies were killing each other on the bank of a red river.
> **Then the sun went out.** They laid down their weapons and went home.

## The idea in one line

**The eclipse is an eye, and it winks.**

The "vacant eye of a god" from the lyric is drawn literally: the Moon is the pupil and the corona is the iris.
For four minutes the film is a war epic watched by that eye. In the last seconds we find out whose eye it is.
Anime Jade sits at a terminal running Earth as a simulation, turns to camera and winks. Her eyelid closes
over her iris the way the Moon closed over the Sun. The wink and the eclipse are the same image.

Under the joke is a sincere thesis that tech Twitter will feel without being told: **look up.**
This is the first time in recorded history that people stopped killing each other because they looked at the sky.
It is also the first time anyone predicted the sky: Thales called it. The film runs the line forward,
from fearing eclipses, to predicting them, to using them to prove relativity, to watching them from the Moon and Mars.
*"What we do next is how history unfolds."*

## Structure: the four contacts of an eclipse

An eclipse has four contact points. They are the film's act breaks, so every genre switch has a physical cause.

| Contact | What happens in the sky | Song | World / material | Genre |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **C1 · first contact** | the Moon takes its first bite out of the Sun | intro → verse 1 → pre-chorus → chorus 1 | **BRONZE**: oil paint | prestige war epic |
| **C2 · totality** | the last bead of sun vanishes; the corona appears | **Drop 1** | **CORONA**: light | sci-fi; reality breaks |
| (mid-totality) | the world holds its breath | breakdown → verse 2 | **MARBLE**: stone | art film; time paused |
| **C3 · third contact** | the diamond ring: *"a sudden spark"* | final chorus | **GOLD**: paint in sunlight | triumphant epic |
| **C4 · fourth contact** | the Sun is whole again; the event ends | **Drop 2** → outro | **ORBIT** (light, cosmic scale) → **ROOM**: ink/cel | cosmic montage → anime comedy |

The sun's growing bite is a **countdown clock** that runs through Act I. Viewers can read it without being told.
Totality lands exactly on the first kick of Drop 1.

## One flow field, four materials (why the switches cohere)

Every plate gets analysed into the same structure: a flow field (the direction of form), edges, tone, depth and mattes.
Each world draws **the same field in a different material**:

* **BRONZE — paint.** Oil strokes laid along the flow field. Caravaggio tenebrism: a near-black umber world with one raking pool of light.
  Altdorfer's sky over everything. Drawn on twos (12 fps), so the strokes boil like *Loving Vincent*.
* **CORONA — light.** The same field drawn as luminous field lines on black, the way the real corona's streamers trace the Sun's magnetic field.
  Warriors become plasma. Depth turns the painting into a 3D scene and the camera orbits it, which a painting can't do. That is the moment reality breaks.
  Drawn on ones (24 fps): machine-smooth after twelve frames a second of hand-made boil.
* **MARBLE — stone.** The same field as marble veining. The battle is frozen mid-strike: statues, suspended arrows, birds stopped in the air, river spray hanging like glass.
  Lit only by the corona above and the orange 360° horizon glow of totality.
* **INK — the room.** No flow field at all: clean lines and flat cel colour, still and quiet. The "real" world is the most stylised one.

**Transitions always pass through the disk.** The Moon's black disk grows until it fills the frame, then opens on the next world.
The eclipse is the wipe.

## Colour script

* **No blue until Earth.** Act I is umber, ochre, vermilion, bronze and lead white. Totality is black, pearl and hydrogen-alpha red (the pink-red of prominences).
  The first saturated blue in the film is the Earth seen from space in Drop 2. It is the same blue as the circle on the back of Jade's jacket that says RARE EARTH.
* Light gets "strange" in real partial eclipses: shadows sharpen, colours drain and go metallic, and the gaps between leaves project hundreds of tiny crescent suns.
  We use all of it: *"when light went strange."*
* The Kızılırmak ("Red River", the Halys) really is red with clay. In the final chorus the swords fall into red water under gold light.

## Beat sheet (timestamps get locked to the beat grid in SHOTLIST.md)

**Cold open (hook, ~first 5 s).** We open on the payoff image: an eclipsed sun, an eye of black and pearl, over a frozen Altdorfer battlefield.
Carved gold capitals: **THE SUN WENT OUT / MID-BATTLE**. Then the painting **rewinds** with its strokes swirling backwards, through the full bright sun, to
**28 MAY 585 BC · THE RIVER HALYS**. The rewind quietly plants the idea that time here can be scrubbed.

**Verse 1 — C1, BRONZE.** Altdorfer aerial: two armies like seas of spears on either bank of a winding red river. Lyrics hang in the sky on a painted
cartouche, a direct homage to the tablet in *The Battle of Alexander at Issus*. Lydian cavalry with long spears; Median archers in felt caps.
A single shaft of light picks out one duel in the shallows. *"Sun burning on the bronze"*: macro glints on helmets and blades, strikes landing on the beat.
*"when light went strange"*: crescent suns dapple a shield under a poplar. A warrior sees them and looks up.

**Pre-chorus.** Faces turn upward one by one. The sky drains to violet-black and Venus appears. *"A halo in the sky"* is set on a circle around the Sun.
*"the vacant eye of a god"*: extreme close-up of a warrior's eye reflecting the eclipse, two eyes rhyming.

**Chorus 1.** Darkness races in. A Lydian and a Mede, mid-duel, stop and look at each other. A sword drops into the water.
*"Go home to the ones you love"*: a hand opens on a child's knucklebone. Baily's beads, then the last bead goes out. **C2.**

**Drop 1 — TOTALITY, CORONA.** On the first kick the paint flakes off like embers and the world is light. The camera orbits the frozen battle.
**HALO · IN THE · SKY · SKY** slam full-frame on the chops. The eye's pupil pumps with the sidechained strings, and brass stabs invert the frame.
Seen from above, the soldiers become particles, agents in the simulation, and re-form on the beat like a drone show into the ring, then the eye.
For two frames near the end the eye in the sky is an anime eye (rewatch bait).

**Breakdown + verse 2 — MARBLE (time paused).** Piano. The light cools and the particles settle into stone. The camera drifts through the frozen battle like a museum at night.
Lyrics are small italic inscriptions, some cut into the plinths. *"Birds went quiet"*: a flock stopped mid-air. *"what we do next is how history unfolds"*:
the camera tilts up to the planets visible at totality. *"a shadow crossed the hills"*: a replay. The Moon's shadow sweeps across a marble relief map of Anatolia
like a strategy-game board, two tiny armies at the river. Wind moves banners while every body stays frozen.
*"Thales foretold the sun would go dark"*: **Thales**, the only living thing, in painted colour, walks between the statues. Gold construction lines bloom around him:
his theorem (a triangle in a circle, the ring again) and the Saros cycle. He glances at camera; he knows. One small meme card here (see ZEITGEIST.md).
*"Warriors behold … a sudden spark"*: the diamond ring. **C3.**

**Final chorus — GOLD.** The spark ripples outward and marble turns back into flesh and paint as the light reaches it. *"Shadow turned to day"*.
Baroque glory, rays through cloud. *"Throw down your blade"*: a slow-motion rain of bronze into the red river. The two kings meet midstream and clasp forearms
(the blood oath, Herodotus 1.74). *"Home to the ones you love"*: the beat finally comes in. Warriors walk home along the river at golden hour.

**Drop 2 — C4, ORBIT.** **THROW DOWN · YOUR · BLADE · BLADE**: blades slam into the earth like a barcode on the kicks. Then the ring locks to the centre of the frame
and history cascades around it, one era per bar: the Halys → Thales → the Antikythera mechanism (a bronze computer with an eclipse dial) →
1919, Eddington's eclipse proves light bends → a 2024 crowd in eclipse glasses → August 2026 → an astronaut on the Moon watching Earth eclipse the Sun,
a red ring made of every sunrise and sunset on Earth → Phobos, a lumpy potato eclipse over Mars → … the Earth, **the first blue**.

**Outro — ROOM.** Pull back past the Moon and the planets. It's on a monitor. A dark room: star charts, a Yagi antenna against the wall, a 1420 MHz sticker.
**Anime Jade** at a terminal. The scrollback shows how it was done (a scheduled eclipse; "notify Thales (he'll take credit)"). She turns to camera, mischievous,
and **winks**. Her eyelid sweeps across her iris exactly like the Moon across the Sun. Stark minor chord. Black.

## Text on screen

Variance is deliberate. Big monumental type carries the hook and the history. Subtitle-scale type carries the intimate verse.
Giant chopped words carry the drops.

* **Hook + history captions:** carved gold capitals (Cinzel-style inscriptional caps), lit by the scene's own light.
* **Verse 1 / choruses:** big, left half, figures composed right. Sometimes on the Altdorfer cartouche, sometimes on a circle around the Sun.
* **Drops:** ultra-heavy wide grotesk filled with flowing field lines; stutters on the chops; tiny mono HUD in the corners (C2 · TOTALITY · 00:03:41).
* **Verse 2:** small italic serif lower third, or cut into marble plinths.
* **Ending:** terminal mono.

## Sound

The song is untouched. A light ElevenLabs layer is used only where it sharpens the story: distant bronze and horses under the cello in the cold open,
which fall away when the light goes strange. Birdsong that stops on *"Birds went quiet."* Keyboard clicks and one sparkle on the wink.
The final chord may be held a few seconds longer (spectral freeze) to give the wink room. Each addition must earn its place in review, or it goes.
